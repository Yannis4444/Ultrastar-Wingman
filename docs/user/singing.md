# Sing a song

Starting a song takes two steps: pick the song, then say who sings it. Ultrastar
Deluxe does the rest.

## Find the song

Open the **Songs** tab. It lists everything Wingman found in your song folders,
with the number of songs at the top.

- The search box matches title and artist while you type. A song id also works.
  The **×** at its right end appears as soon as you type and empties the box
  again in one tap.
- The chips below it narrow the list: **All**, **♥ Favorites**, **Recent**,
  **Sung** and the funnel, **Filter**. Only the chip you are standing on spells
  its name out; the others shrink to their icon so the row keeps fitting a
  phone. The list icon at the end of the row opens the playlists view, where a
  playlist shows its own songs.
- The reload button next to the song count scans the song folders again.
- Tap a song to open it: the cover, a listen-in preview, and the buttons for
  favorite, sing and wish. Below them, **Details** unfolds language, genre,
  year, edition and who made it, and **Add to playlist** puts the song on one of
  the playlists you may edit.
- At the very bottom sit up to three icons, each with a number under it, and each
  opening what it counts. The **trophy** is the best score anybody ever got on
  the song and opens the whole ranking; the **tag** is how many labels the song
  wears and opens the view where you set them; the **speech bubble** is how many
  comments there are and opens them. Tag and speech bubble stand at **0** too —
  putting the first tag on a song and being the first to say something are
  exactly what they lead to — while the trophy waits until somebody has sung it.

## Filter by more than one thing

**All**, **Favorites** and **Sung** are shortcuts. The funnel — **Filter** — is
the long way round, and it is where the tags live.

Tap it and a panel opens with one row for everything the list can be filtered
on, in this order: **Favorites**, **Sung**, the built-in facets — **Duet**,
**Video**, **Genre**, **Language**, **Edition**, **Decade** — and every tag you
are allowed to see. Most rows end in three buttons.

| Choice | What the list keeps |
|---|---|
| **Any** | Everything. That row is not filtering. |
| **Yes** | Only the songs that have it. |
| **No** | Only the songs that do not. |

**Genre**, **Language**, **Edition** and **Decade** end in a drop-down instead,
because there are more than three answers. It offers **Any** and then the values
your own library holds — the genres actually on the shelf, the decades in order.
Pick one and the list keeps the songs with that value; a song whose file names no
genre or no year matches **Any** alone. Those four come out of the song files and
are the same for everybody in the room, account or not.

Set as many rows as you like — they add up, so *Genre: Rock* together with
*Duet: Yes* leaves you the rock duets. The list behind the panel follows every
tap, the song count included, so you can watch it narrow. **Done** closes the
panel and **Reset** puts every row back to where it started.

Most rows start on **Any**. A tag set up to hide its songs starts on **No** —
that is what keeps the put-away songs out of your way. Put its row on **Any**
and those songs come back.

The party can take any of the six facet rows out of the panel — see
[built-in marks and facets](tags.md#built-in-marks-and-facets) — so a filter you
read about here may simply not be offered.

While something is filtering, the funnel is the pressed chip and reads
**Filter · 2**: the number of rows that are not on *Any* — a facet off *Any*
counts like a tag, and so do the tags that were already hiding songs before you
opened the panel. Tapping **All**, **Favorites** or **Sung** throws
the whole filter away and starts again from that chip. **Recent** does not — it
is a different order of the same list, and the filter keeps working inside it.

## What has already been sung

**Recent** lists the songs of the party that is going on right now, the one
played last at the top, with the time it started next to it. A song that was
sung twice appears once, at the later time.

The chip only appears once there is something to show. It counts songs that were
*started*, so a song somebody stopped after one verse is in the list even though
Ultrastar Deluxe never scored it. Six hours without a song end the party, and the
chip disappears again with the next reload — if you are standing on the tab when
that happens it stays put and simply says nothing has been sung yet.

The search box works inside the tab, and tapping a row opens the song as usual —
handy when the room wants the same song again.

## What you have sung before

A small microphone next to a title — blue unless the host recoloured it — means
you have sung that song before, in this party or in any party Wingman ever
recorded. The **Sung** chip filters the list down to those songs.

Both need an account with a player behind it: a song counts as sung by you once
Ultrastar Deluxe scored it under your player. A song nobody scored — stopped
after a verse, or sung with no microphone plugged in — belongs to nobody in
particular, so it does not show up here. It is still in **Recent**.

## The marks next to a title

A row can carry a few small icons after the song's title. They are marks, not
buttons — nothing happens when you tap one, and the song's own view is where the
state is changed.

- A **microphone** means you have sung this song before — the section above.
- A **heart** means it is one of your favorites.
- **Two people** means the file is written as a duet, and a **film camera** that
  the song has a video. Both are off the rows until the party turns them on:
  most libraries have so many of each that the icons would say nothing.
- After those, one icon per **tag** the song wears that was set up to show in the
  list — in the tag's own colour, with its name on hover. A tag nobody chose to
  show stays out of the row and lives in the song's **Tags** view.

The microphone is blue and the heart pink unless somebody changed them: all four
are [built-in marks and facets](tags.md#built-in-marks-and-facets), and whoever
may manage tags picks their colour — or takes any of them off the rows
altogether. Genre, language, edition and year are never marks: they are in the
song's own **Details**, and in the filter.

## Songs you do not see

Some songs are deliberately kept out of the list.

- A tag can be set to **hide songs by default**. Its songs are out of the song
  list — in every chip, not only *All* — and out of the random reel's *All
  songs*, until you put that tag's row on *Any* or *Yes* in **Filter**. They
  are still there: a playlist that holds one still shows it and a wished one
  can still win the reel, because somebody put it there on purpose. An
  **archive** tag, if the party made one, works this way.
- A tag can also take *See songs* away from a role entirely. Those songs do not
  exist for that person at all: not in the list, not in the search, not in the
  reel, and not on a playlist or the wishlist either. The seeded **broken** tag
  works this way, so nobody stumbles into a song that does not play.

If a filter ends up empty because of the first kind, the page says so instead of
pretending you have no favorites. See [Tags](tags.md) for the whole picture.

## Comments

Every song carries a speech bubble at the bottom of its view with the number of
comments under it — **0** included. Tap it and you get what everybody said about
the song, oldest first: who said it, how long ago, and the text underneath.

**Reading is open to everybody.** A guest phone that can see the song list can
read the comments too — knowing that a song is in the wrong key, or that it is
the one the birthday guest wants, is worth more shared than hidden.

**Writing takes an account**, because a comment carries your name and your
picture. Log in and a box appears at the foot of the list. Type, press the paper
plane, and it is there for everybody in the room — the lists other people have
open follow along on their own. **Enter** starts a new line; **Ctrl+Enter**
(**Cmd+Enter** on a Mac) sends, so you never have to reach for the button. A
comment is up to 1000 characters, and there is no editing: say it again, or take
it back.

The host screen is the one exception. It is an administrator, but it is nobody
in particular and has no name to sign a comment with — so it reads the comments
and says nothing. Comment from your own phone instead.

**Taking a comment back** is always yours: the bin at the end of your own row
removes it, after asking. Nobody has to grant you that and no administrator can
take it away. Deleting *somebody else's* comment is the moderator's right —
*trusted* and *admin* hold it by default, and it is the row **Delete anybody's
comments** on [Accounts and permissions](accounts-and-permissions.md). A deleted
comment is gone for good; there is no undo.

If the account behind a comment is deleted, its comments go with it.

## Fix a song's details

Downloaded songs arrive with whatever whoever made them typed: a misspelt
artist, no year, a genre in the wrong language, a title with the karaoke site's
name stuck to it. **Edit details** in the song's view is where that is put
right. *Trusted* and *admin* hold the right by default, and it is the row **Fix
a song's details** on
[Accounts and permissions](accounts-and-permissions.md).

Six things can be changed: **title**, **artist**, **language**, **genre**,
**year** and **edition** — the values that show up in **Details**, in the song
list and in the filter. A title and an artist have to say something; the other
four may be left empty, which means the song simply has no year (or no genre,
or …), and the filter treats it accordingly.

What is written is the **song's own file**, not just Wingman's copy of it, so
Ultrastar Deluxe shows the corrected title too, and the next library scan
agrees with what you typed instead of putting the old value back. Only those
few lines of the file are touched: the lyrics, the timing, the audio, the video
and the cover are exactly as they were, and everything the party did with the
song — its tags, favorites, wishes, playlist entries, comments and scores —
stays with it.

Two answers are worth knowing:

- **"This song's file changed while the editor was open."** Somebody downloaded
  the song again, or edited its file by hand, after you opened the editor.
  Saving would put your older values over theirs, so Wingman does not. Close the
  song, open it again, and you will see what the file says now.
- **"Its file is still open."** Ultrastar Deluxe is holding the song — usually
  because it is the one on the TV. Close USDX (or finish the song) and save
  again. Nothing was written.

A file written in a very old encoding can refuse a letter it has no way to
spell; Wingman says so rather than rewriting the whole file into another
alphabet.

## Delete a song

A song that will never be sung again — a broken file, a duplicate, a download
that went wrong — can be taken out of the library for good. The last row of the
song's view is **Delete song**, and only **admin** holds the right by default.
It is on [Accounts and permissions](accounts-and-permissions.md) as *Delete a
song*, so the host can hand it to **trusted** as well.

Wingman asks first, naming the song, and then deletes **the whole song folder
from the disk** — the txt, the audio, the video, the cover. There is no undo and
there is no bin: the files are gone. Deleting the database row alone would not
help anyway, because the next start would find the folder and read it back in.

What stays is everything the party *did* with the song:

- The **scores** and the parties they were sung at. A score of a deleted song
  still counts towards a singer's record; it simply has no song to name any more.
- The **comments** and the **tags** on it, the **favorites** that pointed at it
  and its place in a **playlist**. None of them shows up anywhere while the song
  is gone — and if the very same song is downloaded again from USDB, it comes
  back under the same name and finds all of it again.

The **wishes** are the exception, and they go: a wish is "somebody should sing
this tonight", and after the file is gone nobody can.

A deletion can be stopped, always with a sentence saying why: the song is
**playing right now** (stop it first), Ultrastar Deluxe still has its files open
(close it and try again), or the song's folder is — or holds — your songs
folder itself rather than being one song inside it, which would take the whole
library with it. In every case nothing at all is deleted.

## Choose the players

Press **Sing** in the song details. The player selection opens.

1. The coloured boxes are the microphone slots, in the order Ultrastar Deluxe
   uses them. The first slot is blue, the second red, and so on.
2. Tap a player below to put them on the next free slot. Tap them again to take
   them off. Tap a slot first to fill exactly that slot.
3. With more than twelve players there is a **Filter players** box above the
   list. Type any part of a name and only the matching people stay visible.
   Clearing the box brings everybody back. Filtering only hides rows — nobody
   loses their slot by being filtered away.
4. Missing somebody? Type a name into **New player** and press **Add**. The new
   player goes straight onto a slot.
5. Press **Play**.

Ultrastar Deluxe opens on the TV with that song, and with the chosen names,
colours and avatars already set up. At least one slot needs a player.

!!! note
    Your slot assignment is remembered in your browser. The same group does not
    have to pick again for every song.

Somebody [hidden](accounts-and-permissions.md#manage-players-and-accounts) is not
in this list, and a stored slot does not bring them back either — the same as for
a player who was deleted.

## Players, colours and avatars

Players live under **User → Players & accounts**, in two groups: the people with
an account, and the guests who only sing.

- **Add** a player by name, under **Guests**. Names are unique.
- Tapping a player's picture uploads a new one — jpeg, png or webp, up to 5 MB
  and 50 megapixels. Ultrastar Deluxe shows it next to the name while singing.
  Your own picture can also be changed by tapping it on the **User** tab.
- The **eye** hides somebody for the evening: they drop out of the player
  selection, and out of nothing else. See
  [Manage players and accounts](accounts-and-permissions.md#manage-players-and-accounts).
- The bin removes a guest. Scores that player already earned keep the name.
  Somebody with an account is removed by deleting the account, which turns them
  into a guest first.

A registered account automatically has a player of the same name, which is why
they stand under **Accounts**. Somebody who sang all evening as a guest keeps
their scores when they register under that exact name later.

The colours are the sixteen Ultrastar Deluxe player colours, and Wingman uses the
same order Ultrastar Deluxe does. There is nothing to configure.

## While a song plays

**Home** shows what is playing; the host screen also shows how far in it is and
who is on which colour. On **Home** the running song takes the place of the
Wingman title at the top of the page — cover, title and artist, with the buttons
below — and hands the title back when the song ends. The time and the singers
are not repeated there: the Ultrastar Deluxe screen in the same room already
shows both. The tile keeps its size either way, so nothing under it jumps.

- **Stop** is the large pink button in the middle, and it asks before it ends
  the song. Only somebody who may stop a song sees it at all. The heart and the
  wish button beside it do the same as in the song's own view.
- Starting another song while one is running offers to replace it — replacing
  stops the running one first. Whoever may not stop a song is told to ask the
  host instead.
- The scores appear on the **Scores** tab shortly after Ultrastar Deluxe closes.
  Wingman reads them from Ultrastar Deluxe and files them under the players it
  put on the slots.
- The host screen holds the results back for a moment first and throws up any
  new [achievements](achievements.md): one singer at a time, their badges flying
  in with the name and the line under it. A song where nobody earned anything
  goes straight to the results.

!!! warning
    An unfinished song scores nothing. Ultrastar Deluxe only writes a score when
    a song is sung to the end, so a stopped song leaves no result behind.
