# Wishes, favorites and playlists

Three ways to remember a song, for three different purposes.

| | Who sees it | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wish** | everybody | "Somebody should sing this tonight" |
| **Favorite** | only you | Your own songs, party after party |
| **Playlist** | you, the people you share it with, or everybody | An ordered set of songs |

## Wish for a song

Open a song and press **Wish**. That is all — no account needed. The phone
remembers who wished what.

The wishlist on **Home** shows every wished song, the most-wanted first, with how
many people want it. Wishing for a song somebody already wished for raises its
count. **Remove wish** takes back your own wish and leaves everybody else's
alone.

The host has a bin beside the rows instead: it takes the song off the wishlist
for everybody at once, for the wish the party is not going to sing. It needs the
right to manage the wishlist, which **trusted** and **admin** hold by default,
and it never appears where your own wish is the only one — **Remove wish** beside
it already does exactly that.

Starting a song takes it off the wishlist. The wishes of the singers who have
an account are settled, and so is every wish made without an account — such a
wish belongs to a phone, not to a player, so the singer's own cannot be told
apart from anybody else's, and the song has just been sung either way. A wish
from another account stays: that person still has not sung it.

!!! note
    Made an account later in the evening? The wishes from that browser move over
    to your account when you log in. Nothing is lost.

## Favorites

Favorites need an account. Log in on the **User** tab, open a song and tap the
heart. A small heart marks your favorites in the **Songs** list, and the
**♥ Favorites** chip filters the list down to them.

Nobody else can see your favorites, and there is no right for the host to grant —
they are yours.

## Playlists

A playlist is a named, ordered list of songs: the birthday set, the ballads,
everything for the late shift. Playlists belong to an account.

Open them from the list icon at the end of the chips row on the **Songs** tab.
The page sorts everything you can see into groups:

| Group | What is in it |
|---|---|
| **Your playlists** | The ones you made |
| **Shared with you** | The ones somebody named you on and you kept |
| **Global** | The ones everybody in the party can see |
| **Hidden** | Shared lists you unfollowed — folded away, one tap from coming back |

A group that has nothing in it is not shown at all. A row carries the first
covers of the playlist as a small mosaic, its name, how many songs are on it and
how long they run, and — where the list is not yours — who it belongs to. A
globe beside the name means everybody can see it. Tap the row to open the
playlist.

### Make one

1. Press the **+** at the top right of the playlists page.
2. Give it a name, a description if it needs one, and decide whether it is
   **global**.
3. Press **Create**. Wingman opens the new playlist so you can start filling it.

### Inside a playlist

Under the name stands one line saying how big the list is, whether it is global,
who shared it and what you may do with it. Below that are the buttons the list
gives you — you only see the ones you may use:

- **Add songs** searches the whole library. Tap a song to put it on the list,
  tap it again to take it off.
- **Edit** turns every row into a reorder row: drag it by the grip, or move it
  with the two arrows, and the **✕** takes the song off the list. The new order
  is saved as you make it. Press the button again when you are done.
- **Follow** / **Unfollow** decides whether a list somebody shared with you sits
  on your playlists page. Unfollowing only hides it under **Hidden**, where
  **Follow** brings it back; the access stays and nobody is told.
- **Save a copy** takes a copy of any playlist you can see — same songs, same
  order, owned by you and shared with nobody.
- The dice at the top right spins [the reel](the-spinner.md) over this playlist
  alone, and the gear — on a playlist you manage — opens
  [Manage playlist](#manage-a-playlist).

Songs also go on a list from the song itself: **Add to playlist** in a song's
details lists the playlists you may edit — tap one to add the song, tap it again
to take it out. A check marks the lists that already have it. **New playlist…**
at the foot of that list makes a new one, private, and puts the song straight
into it — the shortcut for a list that does not exist yet.

### Manage a playlist

The gear at the top right of a playlist opens **Manage playlist**. It is there
on every list you may manage: the ones you made, the ones somebody shared with
you as an **admin**, and — for accounts that may moderate playlists — any list
they can already see.

**What the list is.** Change the **name** and the **description**, and tick
**Global** to show the list to everybody in the party, guests without an account
included. Being able to see a list is not being able to change it: nobody gets
that from the tick. **Save** sends only what you actually changed.

**Who is on it.** Under **Members** stands the owner first, then everybody the
list was shared with. Type the first two letters of a username into **Add
people** to find an account — the search matches the beginning of a name, not
the middle; the **+** beside a name shares the list with that person as a
reader. After that, the dropdown on their row decides what they may
do and the **✕** takes them off the list again — their access goes, the songs
stay.

| Access | What it allows |
|---|---|
| **Read** | See the list and its songs |
| **Write** | Add songs, take songs off, change the order |
| **Admin** | All of that, plus renaming, sharing and deleting the list |

A list you share turns up under **Shared with you** on the other person's
playlists page, and they keep or hide it with **Follow** / **Unfollow**.

**Delete playlist**, at the foot of the page, asks once and then takes the list
away from everybody it was shared with. Only the list goes: the songs stay in
the library, and so does everything the statistics recorded.

Beyond the people named on a list, whoever holds the right to moderate playlists
may change any list they can already see — the global ones, and the ones shared
with them — and **trusted** accounts and administrators hold that right by
default. It never reaches a playlist nobody shared with them: such a list is
invisible to everybody but its owner, and what cannot be looked at cannot be
changed either.

!!! warning
    Deleting an account takes its wishes, favorites and playlists with it — but
    a playlist with an **admin** member is handed to that person instead of
    being deleted, which is what admin access is for. Its scores stay in the
    statistics.
