# Wishes, favorites and playlists

Three ways to remember a song, for three different purposes.

| | Who sees it | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wish** | everybody | "Somebody should sing this tonight" |
| **Favorite** | only you | Your own songs, party after party |
| **Playlist** | you, or everybody if shared | An ordered set of songs |

## Wish for a song

Open a song and press **Wish**. That is all — no account needed. The phone
remembers who wished what.

The wishlist on **Home** shows every wished song, the most-wanted first, with how
many people want it. Wishing for a song somebody already wished for raises its
count. **Remove wish** takes back your own wish and leaves everybody else's
alone.

!!! note
    Made an account later in the evening? The wishes from that browser move over
    to your account when you log in. Nothing is lost.

## Favorites

Favorites need an account. Log in on the **User** tab, open a song and tap the
heart. A small heart marks your favorites in the **Songs** list, and the
**♥ Favorites** chip filters the list down to them.

Nobody else can see your favorites, and there is no right for the host to grant —
they are yours.

## Playlists

A playlist is a named, ordered list of songs: the birthday set, the ballads,
everything for the late shift. Playlists belong to an account.

Create one from **Songs → Playlists**:

1. Press the **+** at the top right of the page. On a page with no playlists yet
   the form is already open — the **+** folds it away again.
2. Give it a name.
3. Decide whether it is **shared**.
4. Press **Create**. The form stays open with the cursor in it, so the next
   playlist is one word away.
5. Add songs from a song's details: **Add to playlist** lists your playlists —
   tap one to add the song, tap it again to take the song out. A check marks
   the playlists that already have it.

The **Playlists** view lists every playlist you can see with the number of songs
on it, and who shared it when it is not yours. Tap a row to browse its songs —
tap a song there to open it, and the owner can take a song out with the ✕. On
your own playlists the pencil renames one (and turns sharing on or off) right in
the row, and the bin deletes it.

### Shared playlists

A shared playlist is visible to everybody in the party, including guests without
an account. A private one is visible to nobody but its owner.

Only the owner can change a playlist — with one exception: an administrator may
fix anybody's. Deleting a playlist deletes only the list, never the songs.

!!! warning
    Deleting an account deletes its playlists, wishes and favorites with it,
    shared playlists included. Its scores stay in the statistics.
